Marjorie Stewart Joyner · Historical research

Biography & Professional Life

An inventor working within the world of American hairdressing and beauty education.

Marjorie Stewart Joyner (24 October 1896 – 27 December 1994) was an American hairdresser, salon owner, inventor, educator and advocate for professional opportunities in the beauty industry. Her career combined technical experimentation with a long commitment to vocational education and community organisation.

1896–1916 · Virginia and Chicago

Joyner was born in Monterey, Virginia, on 24 October 1896. According to the National Archives exhibition, she moved to Chicago in 1912. In 1916 she became the first Black graduate of the A. B. Moler Beauty and Culture School in Chicago. She subsequently opened her own beauty salon.

Professional training and the Walker network

Although her early training prepared her for salon work, Joyner sought further instruction in caring for different hair textures. She studied at a beauty school associated with Madam C. J. Walker and developed a lasting professional connection with Walker's organisation. The National Inventors Hall of Fame describes Joyner as a company vice-president and national supervisor of approximately 200 Walker beauty schools, with an affiliation lasting more than fifty years.

Her work should not be reduced to a single patent: teaching, professional standards and access to training were central to her career. For the relationship between her invention and the Walker company, see Joyner & Walker.

1928 · A permanent-waving apparatus

Joyner filed her patent application on 16 May 1928. US Patent 1,693,515, titled Permanent Waving Machine, was granted on 27 November 1928. The complete patent case file survives in the US National Archives, including application papers, drawings and Patent Office correspondence.

The often-repeated story that a pot roast and its metal rods inspired Joyner's invention appears in the National Inventors Hall of Fame and National Archives accounts. It is a biographical tradition, not something established by the technical drawings or the patent's claims. Examine the primary-source patent record.

Professional organisation and civic activity

The National Inventors Hall of Fame credits Joyner with helping shape Illinois cosmetology legislation in 1924. It also identifies her as a founding member of the National Council of Negro Women in 1935, and as a founder or co-founder of organisations supporting Black beauty professionals, including Alpha Chi Pi Omega in 1945 and the United Beauty School Owners and Teachers Association in 1946.

These organisational histories warrant further investigation through their own surviving records. Joyner's career illustrates how salon work, education, business organisation and civil-rights activity intersected.

Later recognition

Joyner died on 27 December 1994. In 2023 she was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in recognition of her permanent-wave apparatus and broader contribution to the beauty profession.

Evidence and image note

The biographical dates and career summary above follow the National Inventors Hall of Fame (2023) and the National Archives exhibition (2021). The 1928 invention is independently documented by the original patent case file. A 1925 photograph of Joyner with students is credited by the National Archives to the Chicago Public Library; it is not reproduced here because separate image-use permission has not yet been established.

Research revision · 3 November 2026

Invention and professional independence

Joyner's technical work included two separate U.S. patents: the Permanent Waving Machine (US 1,693,515, 1928) and the Scalp Protector (US 1,716,173, 1929). Both name the Madam C. J. Walker Manufacturing Company as assignee. See the patent documentation for their filing histories and original claims.

Her role in training professional beauticians was also significant. The Smithsonian identifies Joyner as a vice president of the Walker company and relates the Walker salon network to Black women's entrepreneurship and community organising. Smithsonian exhibition record.
