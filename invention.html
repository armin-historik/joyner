Marjorie Stewart Joyner · Historical research

The 1928 Invention

Understanding the apparatus from the patent, not from later legend.

Joyner's patented permanent-wave machine belongs to the era of electrically heated hair-waving equipment. The patent describes an apparatus intended to apply heat to multiple sections of hair in a controlled arrangement.

Technical principle

The design brings together a support arrangement and multiple heating elements for the treatment of hair. The exact construction and scope of protection should be read from the patent specification and claims, rather than inferred from a modern illustration.

What the patent establishes

  • An invention attributed to Marjorie Stewart Joyner was described in a United States patent granted in 1928.
  • The document contains a written specification, numbered claims and technical drawings.
  • Its existence alone does not demonstrate how many machines were manufactured or used in salons.

Research question

Contemporary trade advertisements, equipment catalogues or salon records would help establish whether and how the design entered professional use. Those findings will be added only when documented.

Related patented accessory

Joyner's second patent, US 1,716,173 (1929), Scalp Protector, describes a separate protective component used near the scalp during permanent waving. It was filed as a divisional application arising from the original 1928 filing. See both patents and their documentary history.
